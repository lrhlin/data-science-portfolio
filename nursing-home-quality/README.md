# Nursing Home Staffing and Quality in Japan

## Overview
This project examines how direct-care staffing levels and workforce stability relate to care quality
in residential nursing homes in Japan.

Staffing regulations are a major policy tool in long-term care systems, yet evidence on their relationship
with quality—especially outside the U.S.—remains limited.


## Data
- National survey data collected in multiple waves
- Linked administrative data from the Japanese Ministry of Health
- Sample: over 1,000 residential nursing homes
- Quality outcomes include physical restraint use and pressure ulcers


## Methods
I estimate the association between staffing levels and quality outcomes using multivariable regression models,
controlling for:
- Resident severity and care-need composition
- Facility characteristics (ownership, size, region)
- Workforce turnover and experience

Robustness checks explore alternative staffing definitions and specifications.


## Key Findings
- Higher direct-care staffing is consistently associated with better quality outcomes
- Lower staff turnover strengthens the staffing–quality relationship
- Substantial heterogeneity remains across facilities, highlighting limits of observable metrics


## Notes
- Analysis emphasizes interpretability and policy relevance rather than prediction
- Some data elements are omitted or aggregated due to access restrictions
